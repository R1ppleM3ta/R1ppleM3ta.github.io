## Definition
Authority deferral is when a leader holds the authority and the clarity to decide, and delays or softens the exercise of that authority to avoid cost.

## Where the term comes from
The term comes from Ripple Meta, the Brisbane leadership practice founded by Ben Hosking, as part of his Sovereign Leadership work. This is Ripple Meta's framing. It is a proposed theory, not an empirically validated finding.

## Signs
- A decision is sent back for more information after the leader already knows what they would decide.
- Consensus is sought on a decision that belongs to one person.
- Wording is softened until nobody is sure what was decided.
- Others are rescued from the consequences of their own decisions.
- The leader waits for permission that the role does not require.

## Cost
A delayed decision does not stand still. It leaves ambiguity in place, and teams learn to wait for the leader instead of acting. The cost of the delay is paid in the meantime, in time, money and risk, usually by someone other than the leader.

## The one-question test
If I already know what I would decide, what cost am I avoiding by not deciding it yet, and who is paying for the delay in the meantime?
